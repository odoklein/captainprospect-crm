SDR workspace · audit01 — Summary
01 · Executive summary

A cockpit pretending to be a dashboard.

An SDR does one thing, hundreds of times a day: decide who to call next, call them, capture what happened, move on. Every screen should shorten that loop. Today the loop is interrupted by navigation, duplicated information and controls that are louder than the call itself.

The audit found no missing feature. It found a missing centre of gravity. The home page reports on the work; the call page performs it; the callback page re-lists what the home page already lists. Nothing in the shell knows that a call is in progress.

“Everything the SDR needs exists. Nothing tells them where to look.”

The proposal is radical in structure, not in technology: one persistent Shift surface that changes mode — Brief, Call, Follow-up — a call bar that stays alive across routes, and a queue that becomes the only list of truth. No API rewrite. No data migration.

Five moves
01
Fold the dashboard into the shift.

Pacing, mission, queue health and follow-ups become one screen state, not four modules.

02
Make the call a mode, not a page.

A focus stage with the dossier, the script and the outcome dock in one eyeline.

03
One follow-up list.

Overdue callbacks, today’s promises and RDV to confirm live in one timeline.

04
A call bar that persists.

Timer, lead, mute and result stay reachable while the SDR reads a fiche or a note.

05
One design language.

Semantic tokens only, one 7-step type scale, one radius family, two densities.

What improves for the SDR

Fewer decisions before dialling; the next action is always the largest object on screen.

What improves for the manager

The shift log becomes a by-product of the work instead of a form filled at 18:00.

What does not change

Routes stay reachable, permissions stay enforced, the queue contract and result codes stay identical.

SDR workspace · audit02 — Diagnosis
02 · Diagnosis

Six failures, all visible from the first minute of a shift.

Ordered by cost to the SDR, not by implementation effort. Every finding is tied to the code that produces it.

F1 — The app is organised by page, the job is organised by shift

F1

Ten destinations across three sections compete with the one action that matters. The two most important screens — home and call — both claim to be the starting point, and both offer a “start calling” button. The SDR must choose an entrance before doing any work.

lib/navigation/config.ts:224–300 — SDR_NAV: 10 entries, 3 groups. app/sdr/page.tsx:531 “Démarrer les appels”, repeated at :726 “Ouvrir la session d’appel” — two identical intents on the same screen.

F2 — The home page reports; it does not launch

F2

Seven blocks before the first dial: greeting, four KPI cards, mission hero, callbacks panel, activity feed, battlecards and pacing. The callbacks list alone is a 480 px scroll container that duplicates the dedicated Rappels page. The most-used surface spends its first screen on numbers the SDR cannot act on.

app/sdr/page.tsx (1,238 lines) — KPI row :543–637, mission card :648–734, callbacks hub :737–1010, battlecards below. app/sdr/callbacks/page.tsx is a second, 1,319-line implementation of the same list.

F3 — The call screen is a monolith with two mental models

F3

3,811 lines, more than 60 useState hooks, 11 effects and three modals in one client component. A “Carte / Tableau” toggle switches between a single-lead workspace and a data table — two different products behind one switch, each with its own keyboard behaviour. Seven controls sit in the header at 13 px.

app/sdr/action/page.tsx — 3,811 lines, more than 60 useState, 11 useEffect, 3 <Modal>, header controls :2792–2837. Shortcuts bound only in card view (:1939–1982), while the home page advertises them as globally active (:724).

SDR workspace · audit02 — Diagnosis, continued
02 · Diagnosis · continued

Three more failures, all in the same direction: the interface accumulated instead of deciding.

F4 — Three design systems in one product

F4

A semantic token layer exists (--ds-*, Tailwind ink / line / surface) and is partly respected by the pace card and the call header. The home page and the call page bypass it with raw palettes and three radii. Headings swing from 9 px labels to 30 px black type; the call page never uses a weight above medium.

Measured: home — 88 zinc- + 64 slate- classes, 10 × rounded-3xl, 19 × font-black, nine 10 px labels. Action — 159 slate- + 57 neutral-, 21 × text-[13px], zero font-black. Same product, two voices. Counts are raw class-token occurrences, taken with a text search over each page file.

F5 — Feedback is loud, state is fragmented

F5

Saving an action replaces the whole screen with a white 80% overlay. The company name on the call screen opens a “coming soon” modal — a dead end in the primary workflow, and the same modal ships unused in the home page. Callbacks are polled globally every 30 seconds by the layout and fetched again by the page, and the follow-up surfaces (Rappels, Mes RDV, Historique) never share a timeline.

components/layout/AppLayoutShell.tsx:83–133 — 30 s polling of /api/sdr/callbacks?limit=500. action/page.tsx:3775–3808 — “Bientôt disponible” modal behind the company name. app/sdr/page.tsx:224–260 — a second copy of that modal, declared but never mounted. action/page.tsx:2776–2786 — full-screen success overlay.

F6 — Placeholder routes and role drift

F6

Some destinations are thin wrappers, others are missing from the menu while present in the codebase. A Booker sees Listes; an SDR does not, though both call from the same queue. Opportunités is in the BD menu, not the SDR one. The information architecture is not a decision; it is an accumulation.

app/sdr/meetings/page.tsx — 7 lines, delegates to a shell. BOOKER_NAV:307–365 includes /sdr/lists; SDR_NAV does not. /sdr/opportunities exists (14 KB) and is absent from SDR_NAV.

The common denominator: each page was designed for itself. None of them was designed for the transition between two calls — which is where the SDR actually spends the shift.

SDR workspace · audit03 — Reframe
03 · The reframe

Stop designing screens. Design the next thirty seconds.

Three questions run through an SDR’s day. Every screen should answer exactly one of them, and never all three at once.

The three questions

1 · Who now?
What is the single best next action, and why?

2 · What do I say?
Context, objection, proof — in the eyeline of the call.

3 · What did I promise?
Capture the outcome and the next date in one gesture.

“The unit of design is not the page. It is the call, and the ten seconds after it.”
Principles
One primary object — the next action occupies more space than everything else combined.Space
The call never leaves — navigation cannot interrupt an active call without an explicit decision.Continuity
One list of truth — a callback exists once, and every surface reads the same record.Truth
Keyboard is a first-class input — the hand on the mouse is a hand off the phone.Speed
Calm by default — colour marks state, never decoration; no pulsing, no all-caps shouting.Tone

SDR workspace · audit04 — The radical move
04 · The radical move

The call bar: a call that cannot be lost.

Today the call exists inside a page. Open a fiche, a script drawer or a booking modal and the timer, the lead and the result dock disappear behind overlays. The SDR navigates back into the call — or, worse, forgets it is running.

The call bar fixes this with a persistent, 60 px region docked to the bottom of the shell. It exists only while a call is active, and it is the same object on every route: lead name, elapsed time, mute, hang up, and a one-key path to the result dock. It is the single strongest signal that the product understands the job.

“A call is not a page you visit. It is a state the application holds.”

Behaviour: starting a call from any surface raises the bar; navigating keeps it; ending opens the outcome dock as a sheet over the current screen, not a new page; the result is written optimistically and the queue advances behind it.

Non-negotiable: the bar never covers the primary action of the screen behind it. Screens reserve 64 px at the bottom when a call is active — the same contract the current pages attempt with pb-28, but driven by state rather than guessed.

SDR workspace · audit05 — Screens
05 · Screen by screen

What changes, and what deliberately stays.

Shift · Brief — before

  • Four KPI cards, one mission hero, a callback list, an activity feed and battlecards.
  • Two “start calling” buttons on the same screen.
  • Pacing is re-implemented inline while a dedicated component already exists.
  • The company name opens a “coming soon” modal instead of the fiche.

Shift · Brief — after

  • One hero: the next lead, why now, the number, and Call (Enter).
  • A 72 px pacing strip: done / expected / delta, no ring, no gradient.
  • “À suivre” list of the next three actions, then the follow-up timeline.
  • Mission and script in a right rail that follows the call, not the page.

Call — before

  • Two-column layout where the left column carries company, contact, channels, warnings, booking and phone.
  • Script hidden in a drawer; result capture at the bottom of a long column.
  • Table view is a different product, with different shortcuts.
  • Success overlay hides the next lead for a beat.

Call — after

  • Three zones: dossier (facts only) · script (steps, objections, proof) · outcome (keys 1–8 + note).
  • One vertical scroll per zone; the call action never moves.
  • Table becomes a sheet over the same data, not a parallel workspace.
  • Save is a 900 ms inline confirmation on the bar; the next lead is already loaded.

Follow-up — before

  • Rappels, Mes RDV and Historique are three pages with three fetch shapes.
  • Callbacks are split by client-side date maths in two places.
  • No shared notion of “a promise made to a prospect”.

Follow-up — after

  • One timeline: Overdue · Today · RDV to confirm · This week.
  • Every row carries the promise, the channel and the last words said.
  • Inline actions: call, reschedule, mark done, hand to BD — no page change.
Kept on purpose: the card/table distinction is not deleted — it becomes a density control inside the queue. The battlecards survive as contextual script objects keyed to the objection stage. The daily report stays where it is, in the top bar, because it is a compliance artefact, not a work surface.

SDR workspace · audit06 — Design language
06 · Design language

One scale. One radius. Two densities.

Product type scale — seven steps
RoleSize / lineWeight · use
Display30 / 34600 · lead name on the call stage
Title22 / 28600 · screen title, one per screen
Section16 / 22600 · card and panel headings
Body14 / 21400 · everything readable
Body strong14 / 21500 · values, lead names in rows
Meta12 / 16400 · timestamps, missions, counts
Micro11 / 14500 · uppercase only for keyboard keys

Today the product range runs from 9 px weekday labels to 30 px black headings, with 10, 11, 13, 18 and 22 px in between. Removing the middle values is the cheapest quality gain. This document uses 8–9 pt only for print annotations.

Colour roles
Navy · structure, call stage
Magenta · selection, AI, focus
Green · completed, on pace
Amber · waiting, callback due
Red · overdue, blocked

State colour is always paired with a label or an icon. No pulsing dots; no colour as the only signal.

Radius & elevation

One radius family: 8 px for controls, 14 px for cards, 20 px for the call stage. Two shadows only: resting (0 1px 2px) and lifted (0 12px 32px, navy 8%). No rounded-3xl, no coloured glows.

Density

Comfort by default: 44 px targets, 12 px vertical rhythm. Compact for the queue: 32 px rows. One toggle, remembered per user; the type scale does not change.

Layout

12-column grid, 1,440 px max width inside the shell, 24 px gutters. The call stage uses a fixed 320 / fluid / 360 split so the outcome dock never moves between leads.

Motion

Two durations: 120 ms for state, 240 ms for panels. No entrance animations on data. The timer is the only continuously moving element on screen.

Accessibility

4.5:1 minimum for all text, 3:1 for focus rings. Focus order follows the call: dossier → script → outcome. Every shortcut has a visible equivalent. The bar is a landmark region with an accessible name, not a floating div.

Copy

Sentence case. Verbs on buttons (“Appeler”, “Enregistrer le résultat”), not nouns. No “Bientôt disponible” in a live workflow: hide it or ship it.

Optimistic by default

Result capture. The dock closes immediately; the row updates in the queue; a 900 ms inline confirmation appears on the bar; the request reconciles in the background and rolls back with the note intact.

Next lead. Prefetch the next two queue items when the call starts, not when it ends. The gap between calls is where the rhythm is lost.

Follow-ups. Reschedule moves the row between groups without a refetch; the timeline is derived from one record, not three endpoints.

SDR workspace · audit08 — Path
08 · Technical path

Decompose the monolith without pausing the team.

The redesign is compatible with the existing endpoints. The work is mostly extraction: four surfaces, one call state, one design layer. Each phase leaves a shippable product.

Phase 1
Design layer & shell

Tokens only (ink / line / surface / primary) on the four SDR surfaces; type scale and radius normalisation; remove the “coming soon” modal; make the company name open the real drawer. No behaviour change.

Phase 2
Shift surface

Replace the home stack with Brief: hero next action, pacing strip, next three, follow-up timeline. Keep the old components behind a flag for one sprint.

Phase 3
Call state & bar

Extract the call lifecycle into useCallSession (context + timer + result mutation). Add the bar to the shell; move the outcome dock from the page bottom to the bar sheet.

Phase 4
Call stage

Split action/page.tsx into CallStage, LeadDossier, ScriptRail, OutcomeDock, QueueSheet. Target: no file above 400 lines.

Phase 5
Follow-up unification

One derivation over callbacks + meetings + actions; retire the duplicate callback logic in the home page; keep the legacy routes as redirects.

Measurement — instrument, don’t guess

No target numbers are proposed here: the baseline does not exist yet. Instrument these five events first, then set thresholds with the team.

call.started · lead, mission, source surface
call.outcome_saved · result, seconds, note length
call.abandoned · reason, elapsed
followup.promise_created · date, channel
shift.gap · seconds between calls

The north-star candidate is time between two saved outcomes, not calls per hour: it captures speed and quality in one number.

Risks. A persistent bar can hide content on small laptops — solve with the reserved 64 px, not with a dismiss button. Removing the dashboard may unsettle managers — keep a read-only shift summary for them, fed by the same events. The queue contract is the most sensitive part: do not change its filters or cooldown rules during the redesign; move the UI around the contract, never the contract around the UI.

What this document is not

It is not a usability test. No user session, recording or survey was analysed; the findings are structural, from the interface as built. It does not estimate development cost, and it proposes no metric targets, because no baseline exists. Where a judgement is aesthetic — radius, weight, colour — it is argued, not measured.
