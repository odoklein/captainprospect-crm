00 In sixty seconds

Otee is not an app. It is the layer under the app: a self-hosted platform that installs open-source models (Mistral, Llama, Qwen, Gemma, DeepSeek, Phi) on infrastructure you control, routes each request to a right-sized model (S 7B / M 32B / L 120B), and gives one screen for models, teams, quotas and audit. It exposes an OpenAI-compatible API and is sold as one fixed licence — no tokens, no seats.

Their pitch is cost and sovereignty. 70% of requests supposedly run on an oversized model, so bills are 10–30× too high; and with Otee no byte leaves your perimeter, no US provider, no CLOUD Act.

The catch for us: cost is not our problem. Our AI spend is a rounding error at current volumes, and our margins are already 94–96%. The real reasons to care are commercial — being able to say "our AI inference runs in France, on our own servers" to agency and public-sector prospects, and being able to sell AI inside the CRM at a flat cost per workspace instead of metering tokens.

So the meeting is not "is it cheaper". It is: can they prove quality on our real French call recordings, do they hand us a platform we can operate and leave, and is the price a licence or a lock-in with a nicer name.

01 What they actually sell

Their words

"Le bon modèle open source pour chaque tâche, déployé chez vous. Sans dépendance, sans surprise sur la facture." Three principles: sovereign, optimal (right-sized model per task), plug & play (deployed and updated automatically, no AI expertise needed).

Product surface: Optimizer (live routing by complexity), control screen (models, access, quotas, audit, chargeback export), one-click deployment on OVHcloud, Scaleway, Outscale, SecNumCloud clouds, your own GPU servers or Kubernetes.

What it means in practice

  • They are infrastructure, not a feature. Their own FAQ: "Otee est la couche en dessous" — it does not replace your AI apps, your tools plug into it.
  • They do not train or own models. Value is in routing + deployment + governance.
  • Someone still runs GPUs, and someone still pays for them. The question is who.
  • Positioning is enterprise IT (DSI, Finance, Legal), not sales teams. We are not their obvious buyer — which is leverage.

03 Why we are even talking: the fit map

Four angles. Only two of them are real for us today.

Weak

Token cost

Their headline. It does not survive contact with our numbers: the "Fiche de RDV" is 150–2,500 generations a month against an all-in infrastructure cost of a few euros per workspace. Even at 10× volume this is not a line that moves the P&L.

If they open with savings, say so plainly and watch how they recover.

Real

Sovereignty as a sales argument

We already sell single-tenant, data-in-EU, private S3 vault. "Inference runs on our own servers, no US API, no CLOUD Act" is a stronger version of a story we already tell — and it matters to agency clients reselling to public-sector-adjacent buyers.

That is a positioning gain, not a cost gain. Value it that way.

Maybe

AI inside the CRM at flat cost

Today every AI call is metered and quota'd per pack. If inference becomes a fixed cost, AI features can be "included" in the white-label Pack 3 and priced per workspace — simpler to sell, easier to defend in a renewal.

Blocked by one question: is the platform multi-tenant with per-workspace quotas?

New burden

Operating GPU inference

We are a CRM team, not an MLOps team. Someone must size GPUs, patch, watch latency, handle model deprecation and explain to a client why a summary was slow at 9am.

Either they operate it (then data leaves our perimeter and the sovereignty story dies) or we do (then it is a new on-call surface). Pin them on this.

05 Question bank

Ask in this order. Each block ends with a question that closes the previous one.

A · Commercial — get the number first

  • What is the annual licence price, all-in, for a deployment of our size?
  • What is the unit — per instance, per workspace, per company, per model?
  • Is there a floor commitment or minimum term? What is the smallest deal you sign?
  • What is metered if nothing is billed per token? Requests, GPU hours, seats, support?
  • What happens at 3× and 10× our volume — new invoice line, or "unlimited"?
  • What is included in onboarding, and what is charged as a project?
  • Do we get OEM / white-label rights to resell this inside our product to our clients?
  • Is the price fixed for 12 or 36 months, and indexed on what?
  • What are the pilot terms: duration, price, data, and who owns the benchmark results?
  • Walk us through the exit clause. What do we keep on day one after termination?

B · Technical — the part their slide deck will avoid

  • Exact GPU footprint for the S/M/L tiers: model, VRAM, count, and minimum viable setup.
  • Full model list with licences — Llama community licence, Qwen, Gemma: commercial use cleared?
  • How does routing actually decide? Classifier, heuristics, eval scores, learned policy?
  • Can we pin a model per task and bypass the Optimizer when we need determinism?
  • What is p95 latency for 7B and 32B on your reference hardware, at our concurrency?
  • Tool calling and JSON mode reliability — we drive a strict tool loop with schema validation.
  • Which OpenAI endpoints are truly compatible: chat, embeddings, audio transcription, vision, streaming?
  • Failover when a model OOMs or a node dies — automatic, and how fast?
  • "Updated automatically and continuously": who approves a model version change in production?
  • Is the platform multi-tenant with per-workspace quotas we can map to our clients?

C · Security, compliance, data

  • Where does inference physically run — which region, which provider, and can we see it in writing?
  • The control plane: hosted by you or by us? If by you, what telemetry leaves our perimeter?
  • Do you have a DPA, a sub-processor list, and a GDPR record of processing we can attach to ours?
  • Any ISO 27001 / SOC 2, or a recent pen test report we can read?
  • Who at Otee can technically access our infrastructure and prompts, under what process?
  • Audit log: what is recorded, where is it stored, how long, and can we export it?
  • Incident notification: within how many hours, and who writes the client communication?
  • Confirm in writing: our data is never used to train or evaluate any model.

D · Operations and continuity — the question nobody asks

  • Who is on call when inference breaks at 8am on a Monday — us or you?
  • Who plans GPU capacity when our usage doubles?
  • What happens when a model is deprecated upstream? Who migrates, how long, at what cost?
  • Is there a runbook we can hold, and deployment artefacts (Helm, Terraform) we can keep?
  • If Otee shuts down, what do we own and what stops working the next day?
  • Support: hours, languages, channel, response SLA, and who escalates to the founders?
  • How many production deployments are live today, and for how long?

E · Fit with our product — the only questions that decide the deal

  • Can we provision a workspace via API when we onboard a new client, automatically?
  • Can we meter usage per client and export it for our own chargeback?
  • Can the control screen be white-labelled for our Pack 3 agency clients?
  • How do you handle prompt injection in content our prospects and clients write?
  • What do you do that a single vLLM container plus LiteLLM does not? Be specific.

06 Phrases that should worry you

"It's automatic, you don't need to think about it."Automatic routing means invisible decisions. Ask for the routing log per request.
"It's open source, so there's no lock-in."The models are open. The router and the control plane are the lock-in. Different sentence.
"We'll handle the infrastructure for you."Then your data sits in their account, and the sovereignty argument disappears. Pick one.
"It's unlimited."GPUs are never unlimited. Someone pays later, usually at renewal, usually you.
"That's on the roadmap."Ask: "live today, or roadmap?" then write the answer down next to the feature name.
"Our licence is bespoke."Means the price depends on how much they think you can pay. Give them your frame first.
"Small models are just as good now."For summarising a clean document, often yes. For accented French call audio and BANT extraction, prove it.
"We're SecNumCloud."Ask which provider, which qualification, and whether your deployment is actually inside it.

07 Our stance in the room

Frame the deal before they frame it

  • Open with our problem, not their product: "We sell a sovereign, single-tenant CRM to agencies. Our question is whether AI inference can sit inside that promise without adding an ops team." That makes the conversation about their weakest point, not their strongest slide.
  • Never quote our volumes or margins. Say "a few thousand AI operations a month" and let them price blind.
  • Make the pilot the deliverable of the meeting: 30 days, 20 real recordings, blind scoring, written success criteria, one fixed price.
  • Ask for the OEM angle early. If they say no to white-label resale, the product opportunity dies and only the sovereignty story remains — a much smaller deal.
  • Do not commit to anything verbal. "We'll send the benchmark protocol" is the only correct exit line.

Walk away if, by the end of the call

there is no number · no DPA or hosting answer · no named reference customer · no acceptance of a blind test on our recordings · or "we operate the infrastructure" combined with "your data never leaves your perimeter". Any one of these is enough to stop.

09 45-minute agenda

  • 0–5Them first. "Walk us through a real deployment, end to end, on a customer that is live." Listen for what they skip: GPUs, ops, price.
  • 5–15Our context, ten minutes max. Sovereign single-tenant CRM, agency clients, AI on call recordings. No volumes, no margins.
  • 15–25Section 04 claims. Take the three deal-breakers: price unit, GPU BOM, quality proof. Do not move on until each gets a concrete answer.
  • 25–35Live routing test. Hand them five of our real prompts and a call transcript; ask them to route and show the log. Watch the screen, not the slide.
  • 35–42Commercial frame. OEM rights, pilot terms, contract length, exit. Anchor: fixed annual licence, pilot before signature.
  • 42–45Close with a request, not a feeling. "Send the DPA, the model/licence list, two references and a written BOM by Friday."
